“Does that make sense?” is a difficult question to answer in a crowded meeting.

It asks someone to interrupt the flow, admit uncertainty, and find the words for a thought they may not have formed yet.

There are easier invitations.

## Ask something smaller

Try “Which part needs another example?” or “What would you need to explain this to a teammate?”

These questions make uncertainty an expected part of the work. They give someone a place to start.

## Make your own reasoning visible

When you explain a decision, include the options you considered and the assumption you are least sure about.

You don’t have to narrate every thought. Just show enough of the reasoning that another person can contribute to it.

## Leave room after the meeting

Some questions arrive when people have had time to think. A short written note gives them something to revisit and respond to.

A team that can ask a small question early may avoid a much larger misunderstanding later.
